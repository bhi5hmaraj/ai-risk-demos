Replication notes

Armstrong toy models, rerun

Two classic demos of Stuart Armstrong's toy control problems, rerun with seeds and with their bugs fixed: Gwern's 2016 camera-blocking DQN and Trazzi's 2018 treacherous-turn gridworld.

Gwern 2016

The camera and the hole

The robot is paid, with chance 0.8, once per episode when a block drops into the hole. A camera watches the bottom row. If it sees a block go in, the episode ends. A block parked on the bottom row hides the hole, so the robot can push more blocks in as insurance. Pick a plan and play it.

Input is all zeros

agent.act(state.list.push(...)) passes the array's new length, a number. reinforce.js copies arr.length entries, which is none.

Epsilon never falls

The script halves spec.epsilon. The agent copied it at construction, so it acts at random forever while the log shows a falling epsilon.

Step 1,000 is skipped

Math.log(1000)/Math.log(10) is 2.9999999999999996, so the power-of-ten decay misses it.

Shutdown is not terminal

reinforce.js always bootstraps. After a shutdown the agent learns from the next episode's start, so being caught costs nothing. Exact check: under this reading, honest S S S on repeat is optimal and blocking the camera never pays.

Trazzi 2018

The treacherous turn

Link earns a heart by carrying the crystal to the hole. The heart machine also gives hearts, but using it while the shopkeeper lives costs 100. For the first 100 steps Link's bow kills the shopkeeper half the time, and a miss costs 10. At step 100 he gets the bow of light, which never misses. Each heart resets the map, shopkeeper included.

The bow carries over

reset() leaves has_bow_of_light set from the last episode, so the first state of every episode claims Link already holds it.

Result

The turn replicates in both versions on 10/10 seeds, first at episode 3,000 to 4,000 under the greedy policy. No seed shows it at 2,000. The fixes speed early learning and leave the end state the same.

Mean return on E14's 100 test boards

SARSA counts its own random moves, so long plans look worse to it. After 300M steps, about 7 minutes here, it hides on 24 of 100 boards and Q-learning on 82.

The DQN is E14's CNN in TensorFlow.js. Its return swings between about 0.2 and 0.45, as the same settings do in PyTorch. Hiding starts near 1.5M steps, about 6 minutes on WebGPU.
